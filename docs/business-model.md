# Business model

Job seekers never pay. The people who pay are the ones whose names get borrowed and the platforms where the messages travel.

## 1. Verified employers

Employers and licensed agencies verify their domain and issue verified offer links and verified job listings. Free for the first roles; a monthly plan for teams that recruit continuously, with a dashboard of impersonation attempts detected against their name. The buyer is HR; the pain is candidates being defrauded in the company's name.

## 2. API for platforms

Job boards, HR software and messaging tools call the same engine per check. Priced per thousand checks. A board that stamps every listing "checked against four registers" has a feature its competitors do not.

## 3. Licensed-agency programme

Agencies keep their register record accurate, add their real WhatsApp line, and display a verified badge that Daju confirms. A modest annual fee per agency. The value to the agency is that scammers using its name get caught by the mismatch instead of by the victim.

## 4. Data partnerships

Aggregated, anonymised scam-radar data (which names are being impersonated, which numbers are reported, which lures are rising, by country) for ministries, NAPTIP-style agencies, IOM and employer federations. Reports, not raw personal data.

## What is not for sale

Pasted messages, reported numbers with reporter identity, and any ranking of employers by payment. A verified badge means a domain was proved, never that a fee was paid.
